# The name Shamaran

Shamaran takes its name from **Şahmaran**, the legendary human-and-serpent figure
of Kurdish mythology, spirituality, oral tradition, and visual culture. The figure
carries themes of knowledge, healing, protection, nature, resistance, and the
consequences of betrayal—ideas that fit a project designed to remember, help, and
act within clear boundaries.

Şahmaran has a deep place in Kurdish cultural memory and in the material culture of
Kurdish-speaking communities. Academic work describes her as a Kurdish snake
goddess and documents Şahmaran images in Kurdish textiles, rugs, jewelry, glasswork,
metalwork, and homes. Kurdish anthropologist Dilşa Deniz interprets Şahmaran as a
vestige of living Kurdish spirituality and the Mother Earth Goddess of Kurdistan.

The software uses the spelling **Shamaran** for an internationally readable project
name. Its name and official visual identity deliberately honor Kurdish heritage,
Kurdistan, and the Kurdish people who preserved this story and image across
generations.

## Cultural care

Contributions should treat the name, symbol, and cultural references with respect.
Avoid detaching Şahmaran from her Kurdish context, reducing her to an exotic
decoration, or erasing the Kurdish communities that preserved her story and image.
